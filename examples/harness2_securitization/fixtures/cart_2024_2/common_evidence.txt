L540: **[ARTICLE VI --- CREDIT ENHANCEMENT]{.underline}**
L541: 
L542: **[Section 6.01 --- Overcollateralization]{.underline}**
L543: 
L544: The primary form of credit enhancement for the Notes is overcollateralization, which is the excess of the Pool Balance over the Outstanding Note Balance. The initial OC Amount as of the Closing Date is \$24,800,000, representing the excess of the Initial Pool Balance of \$712,300,000 over the Initial Note Balance of \$687,500,000. As a percentage of the Initial Pool Balance, the initial OC Amount represents approximately 3.48% (\$24,800,000 / \$712,300,000 = 3.481%).
L545: 
L546: The Target OC Amount, as of any date of determination, equals 5.25% of the Pool Balance as of the last day of the related Collection Period. Available Funds will be applied to build overcollateralization toward the Target OC Amount through the sequential principal payment structure and, in the event of a Trigger Event, through the turbo principal provisions set forth in Section 5.01(b).
L547: 
L548: The Minimum OC Amount, as of any Determination Date, equals 1.50% of the Pool Balance as of the last day of the related Collection Period. The Minimum OC Amount is calculated based on the current Pool Balance and will therefore decrease in dollar terms as the pool amortizes. A breach of the Minimum OC Amount (i.e., the OC Amount falling below 1.50% of the current Pool Balance) constitutes a Trigger Event under Section 5.01(d)(c).
L549: 
L550: **[Section 6.02 --- Reserve Account]{.underline}**
L551: 
L552: The Reserve Account provides a second form of credit enhancement for the Notes. The Reserve Account was initially funded on the Closing Date in the amount of \$6,875,000, which equals 1.00% of the Initial Note Balance of \$687,500,000.
L553: 
L554: The Reserve Account Required Balance, as of any date of determination, is the greater of (a) 1.00% of the Outstanding Note Balance as of such date and (b) 0.50% of the Initial Note Balance, which amount equals \$3,437,500. The two-prong test ensures that the Reserve Account maintains a minimum floor balance of \$3,437,500 regardless of the amortization of the Notes.
L555: 
L556: Draws from the Reserve Account may be made on each Payment Date to cover shortfalls in Available Funds for the payment of the Servicing Fee, the Indenture Trustee Fee, and interest on all classes of Notes (i.e., priorities First through Seventh of the Section 5.01 waterfall). Replenishment of the Reserve Account is effected from Available Funds at step Thirteenth of the Section 5.01 waterfall, which is the thirteenth priority in the distribution of Available Funds and follows all principal distributions to all classes of Notes (steps Eighth through Twelfth). The mechanics of the Reserve Account are set forth in greater detail in Section 4.04 hereof.
L557: 
L558: **[Section 6.03 --- Subordination]{.underline}**
L559: 
L560: The third form of credit enhancement for the Notes is subordination. The Class B Notes and the Class C Notes are subordinated to the Class A Notes (the Class A-1 Notes, the Class A-2 Notes, and the Class A-3 Notes) with respect to both interest and principal payments. The Class C Notes are subordinated to the Class B Notes with respect to both interest and principal payments. Principal payments are made on a sequential basis, with all principal payments applied first to the Class A-1 Notes, then to the Class A-2 Notes, then to the Class A-3 Notes, then to the Class B Notes, and finally to the Class C Notes.
L561: 
L562: The subordination of the Class B Notes and the Class C Notes is intended to enhance the likelihood of timely payment of interest on and ultimate payment of principal of the more senior classes of Notes. The initial subordination percentage for the Class A Notes (measured as the aggregate initial principal amount of the Class B Notes and the Class C Notes divided by the Initial Note Balance) is approximately 19.27% ((\$82,500,000 + \$50,000,000) / \$687,500,000 = 19.27%).

L206: **[Trigger Events]{.underline}**
L207: 
L208: The transaction documents provide for certain Trigger Events based on the performance of the Receivables. If a Trigger Event occurs, the payment waterfall will be modified to accelerate principal payments to the most senior outstanding class of Notes and to trap excess spread to build overcollateralization. The occurrence of a Trigger Event could extend the weighted average life of the subordinate Notes (Class B and Class C) beyond their expected maturities. Conversely, the occurrence of a Trigger Event could shorten the weighted average life of the senior Notes (Class A-1, A-2, and A-3). The Cumulative Net Loss triggers and the Annualized Default Rate threshold are described under \"Trigger Events.\" There can be no assurance that a Trigger Event will not occur or that, if a Trigger Event does occur, the resulting reallocation of cash flows will be sufficient to protect Noteholders against losses.
L209: 
L210: **[Overcollateralization Erosion]{.underline}**
L211: 
L212: The overcollateralization amount serves as credit enhancement for the Notes. However, the overcollateralization amount may decline over time if net losses on the Receivables exceed the amount of excess spread available to maintain or build overcollateralization. If the overcollateralization amount declines below the Minimum OC Amount of 1.50% of the initial pool balance (\$10,684,500), a Trigger Event will occur and the payment waterfall will shift to a turbo structure. In a severe loss scenario, the entire overcollateralization amount could be eroded, and losses could be allocated to the Notes in reverse order of priority (Class C first, then Class B, then Class A-3, then Class A-2, then Class A-1). There can be no assurance that the overcollateralization amount, together with the other forms of credit enhancement, will be sufficient to protect any class of Notes from loss.

The interaction between fixed-rate Notes and variable prepayment behavior on the underlying Receivables creates reinvestment risk for Noteholders.

L576: **[Definition of Trigger Event]{.underline}**

L586: **[Annualized Default Rate]{.underline}**
L587: 
L588: The \"Annualized Default Rate\" for any Collection Period is calculated as follows: the aggregate principal balance of Receivables that became Defaulted Receivables during such Collection Period, multiplied by twelve (12), divided by the Pool Balance as of the first day of such Collection Period. For purposes of this calculation, a \"Defaulted Receivable\" is a Receivable as to which any scheduled payment is more than 90 days past due, or as to which the related Financed Vehicle has been repossessed (whether or not such Receivable is 90 or more days past due). The three-month rolling average of the Annualized Default Rate is the arithmetic average of the Annualized Default Rates for the three most recent Collection Periods.

L592: The following table sets forth the Cumulative Net Loss Rate trigger levels applicable to each semi-annual period. A Trigger Event will occur if, as of any Determination Date, the Cumulative Net Loss Rate (calculated as the aggregate net losses on the Receivables from the Cutoff Date through the end of the most recent Collection Period, divided by the Initial Pool Balance) exceeds the applicable percentage for the period in which such Determination Date falls:
L593: 
L594:   -----------------------------------------------------------------------
L595:   **Payment Date Period**             **CNL Trigger (%)**
L596:   ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
L597:   January 2025 -- June 2025           1.25%
L598: 
L599:   July 2025 -- December 2025          2.50%
L600: 
L601:   January 2026 -- June 2026           3.75%
L602: 
L603:   July 2026 -- December 2026          4.75%
L604: 
L605:   January 2027 -- June 2027           5.50%
L606: 
L607:   July 2027 and thereafter            5.75%
L608:   -----------------------------------------------------------------------
L609: 
L610: The Cumulative Net Loss Rate trigger levels are designed to identify deteriorating pool performance at an early stage, allowing the transaction to redirect cash flows to protect senior Noteholders before losses reach critical levels.
L611: 
L612: **[Consequences of a Trigger Event]{.underline}**
L613: 
L614: Upon the occurrence and during the continuance of a Trigger Event, the payment waterfall will shift to a \"turbo\" structure. Under the turbo structure, all excess spread (after payment of fees and interest) is applied to accelerate principal payments to the most senior outstanding class of Notes, and excess spread is trapped to build overcollateralization to the Target OC Amount. During a Trigger Event, no amounts will be distributed to the Certificateholder. The effect of a Trigger Event is to prioritize the repayment of the Class A Notes and to increase the credit protection available to the Class A Notes and the Class B Notes.
L615: 
L616: A Trigger Event is reversible. If, on a subsequent Determination Date, the conditions that gave rise to the Trigger Event are no longer in effect --- specifically, if (i) the three-month rolling average of the Annualized Default Rate is equal to or less than 3.50%, (ii) the Cumulative Net Loss Rate is equal to or less than the applicable trigger level, and (iii) the OC Amount is equal to or greater than the Minimum OC Amount --- then the Trigger Event will be deemed to have been cured, and the payment waterfall will revert to the standard priority of payments described under \"Flow of Funds.\"
L617: 
L618: Investors in the Class B and Class C Notes should be aware that the occurrence of a Trigger Event could significantly extend the weighted average life of such Notes, as principal payments to the subordinate classes may be deferred during the Trigger Event period. Conversely, investors in the Class A Notes may experience a shortened weighted average life during a Trigger Event, as cash flows are redirected to accelerate the repayment of the senior classes.
